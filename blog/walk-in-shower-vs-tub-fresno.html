Walk-In Shower vs. Tub Conversion: Which Is Right for Your Fresno Home?

The question I get asked most by Fresno homeowners — answered honestly, with no agenda.

Andrey Boginski Owner, Andrey's Construction & Remodeling Inc. February 2025 7 min read

Almost every homeowner I sit down with for a bathroom estimate asks this at some point: "Should I just rip out the tub and put in a walk-in shower?" It seems like a simple question, but the right answer depends on your house, your family, and how long you plan to stay.

I've done both — hundreds of times across Fresno, Clovis, and the Central Valley. Here's my honest take, without the sales pitch.

Short answer: If you have more than one bathroom, converting to a walk-in shower almost always makes sense. If it's your only bathroom, keep a tub — for resale value and practical reasons.

The Case for a Walk-In Shower

Walk-in showers have become the dominant choice for master bath remodels in Fresno — and for good reason. Here's why most of my clients end up going this direction:

Daily usability

Most adults shower every day and use the tub occasionally at best. Converting that space to a large walk-in shower with a rain head, a bench, and a niche gives you something you'll actually use every morning. A freestanding tub that nobody uses is just square footage gone to waste.

The space opens up dramatically

Removing a built-in tub surround frees up a surprising amount of floor space. In a standard Fresno master bath — typically 8×10 or so — that conversion can make the room feel 30% larger. Add large-format tile on the walls and you've got a bathroom that feels genuinely luxurious.

Easier to clean and maintain

Tubs collect grime around the edges, under the rim, and along the grout lines of the surround. A well-designed walk-in shower with sealed tile or a linear drain is much easier to keep clean and stays looking newer longer.

Cost is often comparable

A tub-to-shower conversion in Fresno typically runs $6,500–$14,000 depending on the size, tile choices, and whether you're moving any plumbing. That's not cheap — but it's often less than a full bathroom gut remodel, and the visual transformation is dramatic.

The Case for Keeping a Tub

I'm not anti-tub. There are real situations where keeping one makes sense, and I'll tell you that upfront even if it means a smaller project for me.

You have young children

Bathing young kids in a walk-in shower is genuinely awkward. If you have kids under 8 or 10, a tub is still the practical choice for that bathroom — even if you add a nice shower too.

It's your only bathroom

Fresno has a lot of older homes with just one bathroom. If that's your situation, removing the tub will hurt your resale value. Buyers — especially families — expect at least one tub in the house. The conventional wisdom is that a home with no tub can lose $5,000–$15,000 in perceived value, depending on the neighborhood and buyer pool.

You actually use it

Some people really do soak. If you take a bath twice a week, keep the tub. This seems obvious, but you'd be surprised how many people want to remove a tub they use regularly just because walk-in showers look nice on Instagram.

What About Resale Value in Fresno?

The short answer is: walk-in showers add strong resale value in master bathrooms, but only when there's a tub somewhere else in the house. The Fresno real estate market skews toward families — so buyers expect at least one tub in the home. As long as you have that covered, a well-done master shower conversion is a real selling point.

From what I've seen on projects where clients later sold their homes: a high-quality shower conversion with frameless glass and good tile work consistently returns 60–80% of its cost at resale. The key word is "high-quality" — a cheap tub surround replacement doesn't move the needle the same way custom tile does.

My Recommendation

If you're remodeling the master bath and you have at least one other bathroom in the house with a tub — convert it. You'll use it every day, it'll look incredible, and it adds real value. Go with frameless glass, a 48" or larger shower, a built-in niche or two, and a good rain head. That's the combination that holds up and photographs well when you sell.

If it's your only bathroom, keep a tub — but you can still make it beautiful. A new freestanding tub with a floor-mounted filler, fresh tile surrounds, and a separate shower if space allows is a stunning combination that checks every box.
