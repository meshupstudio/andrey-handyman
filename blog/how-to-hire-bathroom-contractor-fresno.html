How to Hire a Bathroom Remodeler in Fresno: 7 Questions to Ask First

Advice from a licensed Fresno contractor — including the red flags most homeowners miss.

Andrey Boginski Owner, Andrey's Construction & Remodeling Inc. March 2025 9 min read

I've been doing bathroom remodels in Fresno and Clovis for over 20 years. And I'll be honest with you about something: not everyone who calls themselves a bathroom contractor in this area is doing things the right way. Some aren't licensed. Some sub out every piece of work. Some give low-ball quotes and find ways to charge more once the walls are open.

You deserve to know how to protect yourself. Here are the seven questions I'd ask any contractor — including me — before signing anything.

California law requires contractors to be licensed for jobs over $500. You can verify any license at cslb.ca.gov in about 30 seconds. Always do this before you hire.

The 7 Questions to Ask

1

Are you licensed with the California CSLB, and can I verify it?

In California, any contractor doing work valued over $500 must be licensed with the Contractors State License Board. A legitimate contractor will give you their license number without hesitation. You can then verify it at cslb.ca.gov — takes about 30 seconds.

What to check: Is the license active? Does it cover the right classification (B - General Building or C-20 Warm-Air Heating for plumbing/HVAC work)? Is there any history of complaints or disciplinary actions?

Red flag: Any hesitation, a number that doesn't check out, or "I work under my boss's license" for solo work.

2

Do you carry general liability and workers' compensation insurance?

If a worker gets injured at your home and the contractor doesn't have workers' comp, the claim can fall on your homeowner's insurance. If they damage something in your home, liability insurance is what covers it.

Ask for a certificate of insurance before work begins — not just a promise. A reputable contractor will provide it without pushback.

Red flag: "I don't need that for small jobs" or delays in providing documentation.

3

Who actually does the work — you or subcontractors?

This question gets overlooked more than any other, and it matters a lot. Some contractors function as project managers — they sell the job, then hire multiple subs to do the actual work. That's not always bad, but it means less accountability, more scheduling complexity, and often less consistency in quality.

I handle tile, plumbing, and electrical in-house. When I hand you my card, that's who shows up. Ask any contractor to explain specifically what work their own crew does versus what they sub out.

Red flag: A vague answer, or "we work with the best subs in the area" without specifics.

4

Can you provide an itemized written estimate?

A legitimate contractor should be able to give you a line-item quote that separates labor from materials, lists specific fixtures or tile by brand/model if you've selected them, and includes a timeline. A quote that just says "Bathroom remodel — $12,000" doesn't protect you from scope creep.

The written estimate is also what becomes the contract. You want it to be specific enough that both sides agree on exactly what "done" looks like.

Red flag: Verbal-only quotes, resistance to putting things in writing, or a contract with large "allowances" that can balloon.

5

Will you pull the required permits?

Permits are required in Fresno for plumbing relocation, electrical work beyond like-for-like replacement, and structural changes. Some contractors suggest skipping permits to save time and cost. This is a bad deal for the homeowner — unpermitted work can create problems when you sell the home, void your homeowner's insurance in some cases, and leave you liable if something goes wrong.

A licensed contractor should pull all required permits as part of the job. The cost is usually modest and it's already factored into a legitimate quote.

Red flag: "We don't need a permit for this" on work that clearly requires one, or asking you to pull permits yourself.

6

Can I see photos of similar projects you've completed in Fresno?

Any contractor with real local experience should be able to show you photos of completed bathrooms — ideally in homes in the same area, similar in age and style to yours. Look for clean tile work, straight grout lines, well-fitted fixtures, and finished details like trim and caulk lines.

Photos from Pinterest or manufacturer websites aren't the same as photos of their own work. Ask specifically for project photos from the last 12–18 months.

Red flag: Stock photos, "our website is being updated," or photos that look professionally staged but not like real homes.

7

What's your payment schedule?

In California, contractors cannot legally request more than 10% of the contract price or $1,000 (whichever is less) as a deposit before starting work. A reasonable payment schedule for a bathroom remodel typically looks like: deposit at signing, a draw at demo completion, a draw at rough-in completion, and final payment at walkthrough approval.

Paying in full upfront is never appropriate. Large cash-only requests are a significant red flag.

Red flag: Asking for 50% or more upfront, cash-only requirements, or pressure to pay before each phase is actually complete.

One More Thing: Trust Your Gut

After 20 years of this work, I've learned that the technical questions matter — but so does how a contractor communicates. Do they show up on time for the estimate? Do they listen to what you actually want, or do they push a standard package? Do they call back?

A good bathroom remodel is a relationship that lasts several weeks. You want someone who treats your home respectfully and communicates clearly throughout. If something feels off in the estimate meeting, it won't get better once the walls are open.
